# Team Code of Conduct

**Status: Draft — awaiting team confirmation**

**Effective date: Not set**

This proposed agreement applies to the COMP3500SEF project team's development,
documentation, reviews, messages, meetings and assessed work. It is intended to
support reliable collaboration and fair recognition of contributions.

Each member should review and confirm the agreement before it is adopted. Record
the actual confirmations, unresolved concerns and agreed effective date using
the decision template in the [project record](docs/PROJECT.md). Update this
document's status through a PR once the team has agreed. This draft records no
signatures or member agreement. Later amendments should follow the same process.

## Respectful participation

- Discuss ideas, evidence and work constructively. Explain concerns clearly and
  offer a practical next step when possible.
- Make room for different experience levels. Ask for clarification, share useful
  context and give teammates a reasonable opportunity to respond.
- Avoid personal attacks, harassment, discrimination, intimidation and ridicule.
  Disagreeing with a technical decision is acceptable; targeting a person is not.
- Respect privacy. Keep sensitive concerns and personal information out of public
  issues, PRs and project records. Do not share another member's private messages
  more widely than needed to address a concern.

## Responsibilities and delivery

- Use the responsibilities in the [project record](docs/PROJECT.md) for ownership
  and coordination. Discuss changes affecting another module with its owner.
- Agree on a task's owner, expected result, acceptance checks, deadline and next
  update. Make commitments that reflect actual availability and dependencies.
- Provide an update at the agreed time. When blocked or likely to miss a date,
  report the issue promptly, explain the impact and propose help, a smaller
  deliverable or a revised date. Record any agreed change in the task.
- Share work early enough for integration and review. Use the
  [contribution workflow](CONTRIBUTING.md), respond to review comments and help
  verify work affecting your module.
- Record completed work with appropriate evidence. Design, review, testing,
  coordination and documentation are contributions as well as code; commit
  counts alone do not describe someone's contribution.

## Communication and records

Use the existing group chat for routine asynchronous discussion. Agree on response
and update expectations within each task rather than assuming everyone is always
available. If a meeting is needed, agree its time and purpose and give notice
when unable to attend.

Record decisions affecting scope, interfaces, responsibilities or dates in the
task, PR or project record, and tell affected members where to find them. Record
only actual attendance, decisions, confirmations and work. Course Logbooks should
be based on this evidence and the official submission requirements.

## Academic integrity and attribution

Follow the official course and institutional requirements for collaboration,
assessment, references and tool use. Cite the sources of reused code, text,
designs, data and other materials, and check the applicable reuse conditions.
State who contributed what; do not present another person's work as your own or
fabricate contributions, test results, meetings or Logbook entries.

Before using AI tools in assessed work, check the course's permission and
disclosure requirements. If the requirements are unclear, seek clarification
from the course instructor or tutor. This draft does not establish permission
or a ban on AI use.

Where use is permitted, record the tool, purpose, affected work and how the output
was checked in the task or PR, along with any disclosure required for submission.
Contributors remain responsible for understanding, explaining and validating the
work they submit.

## Raising and resolving concerns

1. When comfortable doing so, discuss an ordinary disagreement privately with
   the person involved, using specific facts and the change you would like.
2. If the issue persists, or direct discussion is uncomfortable or inappropriate,
   contact the team lead privately. The team lead is identified in the project
   record. Describe the issue, its effect and any relevant evidence.
3. If the team lead is involved, approach an uninvolved member to coordinate a
   fair discussion. A person involved in the concern should not decide its outcome.
4. Seek guidance from the course instructor or tutor when the team cannot resolve
   the concern or when formal assessment or academic-integrity handling is needed.
   Members may approach teaching staff directly for sensitive concerns.

The coordinator should hear the people involved, limit disclosure to those who
need the information and agree practical next steps, an owner and a follow-up
time. Record only the necessary outcome in an appropriate place; omit sensitive
details from public records. Good-faith concerns must not lead to retaliation.

The team may agree changes to its working arrangements. Marks, disciplinary
action and formal course decisions remain with the institution's applicable
processes; this agreement assigns no automatic penalties.
